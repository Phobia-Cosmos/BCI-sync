Figure 2. Illustration of the differences among previous BBDA methods, SFDA methods under the black-box setting, and our proposed
RDKC. (A) Previous BBDA methods primarily focus on optimizing global image-level classification by suppressing negative predictions to
better exploit reliable information. However, they fail to address the core challenges of detection tasks, such as object localization and local
classification optimization. (B) Unlike source-free settings, SFDA methods under black-box constraints suffer from significant performance
degradation. This is mainly due to the static nature of the downloaded predictions, which remain unchanged during the entire adaptation
process. As a result, even advanced source-free optimization techniques are fundamentally limited, leading to only marginal improvements
over the initial fixed outputs. (C) To address these limitations, we propose RDKC: MR suppresses noise using high-confidence regions
while preserving blurred areas to balance noisy label learning and knowledge retention, and SC enhances bounding box perception through
contrastive analysis.

Figure 3. Overview of the whole training of RDKC. According to the BBOD, (a) target data can only be uploaded to the cloud API, and
their corresponding noisy hard predictions must be downloaded without accessing the source model; (b) Subsequently, RDKC employs a
simple knowledge distillation to enable the teacher model to learn target domain knowledge under the supervision of noisy hard predictions;
(c) Based on the Mean-Teacher framework, RDKC freezes the teacher model’s parameters and initializes the student model by sharing the
teacher’s weights. The student model is trained via our joint optimization, and the teacher is updated through EMA. Our joint optimization
comprises two key components: Memory Retention, a BBOD-specific loss function designed to retain informative knowledge under noisy
supervision; and Scene Compression, a region-aware contrastive analysis that enhances fine-grained perception of bounding boxes.
